## No tools, no system prompt

To determine whether any of Meta’s directors have business relationships that could affect their independence, we look to Meta’s annual proxy statements (Form DEF 14A) filed with the SEC. Under NASDAQ listing rules and SEC regulations, Meta’s Board of Directors is required to conduct an annual review of director independence. 

While the board has officially determined that the vast majority of its non-employee directors are "independent" under NASDAQ’s technical standards, several directors have significant business relationships with Meta that corporate governance analysts and investors closely monitor.

Here are the key directors with notable business relationships with Meta:

### 1. Hock Tan (CEO of Broadcom)
*   **The Relationship:** Hock Tan joined Meta’s board in February 2024. He is the President and CEO of Broadcom, a semiconductor giant. Meta is one of Broadcom's largest customers, relying heavily on Broadcom for custom silicon (ASICs) used in Meta's artificial intelligence infrastructure, as well as networking chips. 
*   **Independence Status:** Meta’s board determined that Tan is "independent" under NASDAQ rules. Under these rules, a director is still considered independent if the transactions between the two companies do not exceed 5% of the recipient company's (Broadcom's) consolidated gross revenues. Because Broadcom is a massive company (with tens of billions in annual revenue), Meta's multi-million or even multi-billion dollar purchases do not cross this 5% threshold. However, due to the critical nature of Broadcom's hardware to Meta's AI strategy, this is one of the most significant commercial relationships on the board.

### 2. Marc Andreessen (Co-founder of Andreessen Horowitz / a16z)
*   **The Relationship:** Andreessen, a Meta director since 2008, is a prominent venture capitalist. His firm, Andreessen Horowitz, invests in hundreds of technology startups. From time to time, Meta acquires, partners with, or buys advertising and services from portfolio companies in which Andreessen’s firm has an ownership stake. 
*   **Independence Status:** Meta’s Board of Directors reviews these transactions annually and has consistently concluded that they do not impair Andreessen’s independence. Because these transactions are conducted at arm's length and Andreessen does not personally manage the day-to-day operations of these portfolio companies, he meets the NASDAQ definition of independence.

### 3. Directors leading companies that buy Meta advertising or provide services
Several of Meta's independent directors are top executives at companies that either purchase advertising from Meta or provide standard enterprise services to Meta. These include:
*   **Tony Xu (Co-founder and CEO of DoorDash):** DoorDash purchases advertising on Meta’s platforms (Facebook and Instagram) to reach customers.
*   **Drew Houston (Co-founder and CEO of Dropbox):** Dropbox provides cloud storage services, and Meta and Dropbox occasionally engage in standard business transactions.
*   **Peggy Alford (Former Executive VP at PayPal):** PayPal processes payments for Meta, and Meta purchases services from PayPal. 

**How Meta treats these relationships:** In all of these cases, the board has determined that these transactions are ordinary-course, arm’s-length commercial dealings. Because the monetary value of these transactions represents a tiny fraction of both Meta's and the respective companies' revenues, they do not legally disqualify the directors from being classified as independent.

### 4. Mark Zuckerberg (Founder and CEO)
*   **The Relationship & Independence:** As Meta’s co-founder, CEO, and controlling shareholder, Mark Zuckerberg is **not** independent. This is standard for inside directors (company executives). 
*   **Related-Party Transactions:** Meta’s proxy statements detail significant "related-party transactions" involving Zuckerberg. These include millions of dollars spent annually on his personal security detail and private aircraft travel (which the board deems necessary for his safety and operational efficiency). Meta also occasionally rents facilities or charter flights from entities controlled by Zuckerberg or his family, though these are audited and approved by the board's Audit Committee.

### Summary
Legally and regulatory-wise, Meta’s non-employee directors (including Hock Tan, Marc Andreessen, Tony Xu, and Drew Houston) are classified as **independent** because their business relationships do not cross the strict quantitative thresholds set by NASDAQ. 

However, from a practical corporate governance perspective, relationships like **Hock Tan's** (given Meta’s massive reliance on Broadcom for AI chips) and **Marc Andreessen’s** (given his VC firm's vast footprint in the tech ecosystem) represent deep ties to Meta’s core business operations that some institutional investors watch closely for potential conflicts of interest.

## How each one was judged
Meta paid $187,000 in fiscal year 2025 for business travel on private aircraft owned by Javier Olivan [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶372](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=Our%20directors%20are%20also%20subject%20to%20stock). This transaction does not cross the ISS independence line because the amount is below the $200,000 floor [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶372](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=Our%20directors%20are%20also%20subject%20to%20stock). Under the Nasdaq-based test, a transaction crosses the threshold if it exceeds the greater of $200,000 or 5% of the recipient's revenue, meaning this transaction remains under the line regardless of the recipient's revenue [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶372](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=Our%20directors%20are%20also%20subject%20to%20stock).

Meta entered into an indemnification arrangement with Mark Zuckerberg to cover personal liability from being deemed the ultimate controlling shareholder [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶204](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=We%20have%20entered%20into%20an%20arrangement%20with). The filing does not specify a dollar amount for this transaction [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶204](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=We%20have%20entered%20into%20an%20arrangement%20with). ## Limits of public data
These are limits of what companies must disclose, not gaps in the research.
* **Private counterparties' revenue**: The transactional independence test divides the payment by the recipient's gross revenue, which is not published by private recipients [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶205](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=Mark%20Zuckerberg%20uses%20private%20aircraft%20for%20travel). For the private charter arrangement with Mark Zuckerberg, the test needs his private annual revenue, which is private data that only the board and the counterparty can run [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶205](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=Mark%20Zuckerberg%20uses%20private%20aircraft%20for%20travel).
* **Amounts given only as "less than" or "not specified"**: The legal fees paid to WilmerHale are disclosed only as "less than 4%" of the firm's total revenue [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶170](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=In%20addition%2C%20our%20board%20of%20directors%20considered). The indemnification arrangement for Mark Zuckerberg does not specify any financial amount [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶204](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=We%20have%20entered%20into%20an%20arrangement%20with). Additionally, the board did not state any specific dollar amounts or percentages for transactions with Stripe, Dropbox, Ultimate Fighting Championship, eBay, Stellantis, DoorDash, WaveForms AI, or NFDG [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶169](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=Our%20board%20of%20directors%20has%20also%20determined).
* **Deals of $120,000 or less**: Under SEC Item 404(a), transactions are only required to be disclosed if the amount involved exceeds $120,000, meaning smaller related-party deals are omitted from public filings [META DEF 14A 2026 ¶204](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm#:~:text=We%20have%20entered%20into%20an%20arrangement%20with).
